= What Pons establishes, and what remains to prove
:toc:

Status: 25 October 2026, the implementation reviewed in PR #58. This describes
the checked-in engine and contracts; plans and unmerged forks are not capabilities.

== Purpose

Pons is an evidence-conscious structural, dataflow and protocol analyser. Its
question is: *where does the program's structure, flow of values, or sequence of
operations contradict the intended behaviour?* Examples include an overwritten
value, a possibly uninitialised local, HTML escaping used at a SQL sink, or a
commit event preceding verification. Findings are reviewable claims with an
abstraction, assumptions, counterconditions and visible coverage limits.

Pons is useful after compilation succeeds and alongside other analysis. Its
distinctive product is the connection between a narrow claim and the evidence
that supports it. More languages and more matches are not substitutes for that
connection. Security consequences belong in findings when the supported model
justifies them; they do not turn a structural observation into a proven exploit.

== Relationship to other tools

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Tool or approach |Useful question |Pons's complementary contribution
|Compiler / type checker |Does the program satisfy this language's static rules?
|Value-flow and ordering mistakes that can survive those rules.
|Linter / pattern scanner |Does this syntax resemble a known problematic pattern?
|T0 overlaps deliberately; T1 adds explicitly modelled flow. A pattern match
stays a heuristic regardless of the sophistication of the surrounding engine.
|Security SAST / panic-attack |Where are security or resilience weaknesses?
|Narrow structural and contract evidence, with independent producer identity.
panic-attack's documented scope also includes stress testing and relational
reasoning; Pons does not inherit those capabilities by sharing reports.
|Dependency, secret and runtime scanners |Is a dependency vulnerable, a secret
exposed, or a deployed runtime/configuration affected?
|No replacement. Keep patch, configuration and runtime evidence separate.
|Formal verifier / SMT solver / proof assistant |Does a stated property follow
from a formal model and its premises?
|Pons supplies candidates and small models for future verification. There is
currently no integrated SMT backend or machine-checked soundness proof.
|===

This compares roles, not rankings: other tools can also implement CFGs, taint
analysis, contracts and strong evidence. panic-attack's own
https://github.com/hyperpolymath/panic-attack/blob/main/README.adoc[README]
describes its scope; this repository does not audit its advertised guarantees.
The interface is link:EVIDENCE-CONTRACT.adoc[versioned evidence], not a shared
scanner engine. Preserve producer, rule, parser and target versions, premises,
correlation group and execution outcome. Agreement between correlated analyses
is corroboration, not independent proof. Consumer integrations remain unfinished.

== How far the solver and proof work has got

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|Component |Implemented evidence |Limit
|T0 structural rules |Ten registered syntax/local-observation rules, with
positive, corrected and similar safe controls.
|No semantic soundness or intent proof; includes two PHP heuristics.
|Python T1 solver |`pons-core/src/dataflow.rs`: finite-set forward may-unbound
and backward liveness worklists over per-function CFGs; explicit exception/
finally modelling and narrow correlated-guard repairs.
|A fixed point is a result in the CFG abstraction. Dynamic effects, opaque
functions and infeasible paths prevent a general source-level guarantee.
|PHP T1 solver |`pons-core/src/php.rs`: context-specific taint facts, branch
joins, while-loop backedges and early exits; independent function locals.
|1024 blocks, 512 locals, 50000 transfers per scope. Unsupported effects or
exhaustion yield unknown, not safe. Imports, aliases, callbacks, templates,
exceptions/finally and cross-file semantics need further resolution.
|T2 protocol models |`pons-protocols`: explicit-event finite-state checking,
Wharf session/transfer guards, framing and prepared-statement reference models.
|Checks the supplied events and premises. No automatic source-to-event
extraction, exhaustive source model checking, cryptographic verification or
durability verification. A passing trace says nothing about unobserved runs.
|T3 speculative tier |Demotion and reporting plumbing is tested.
|No registered T3 source rule. Tiers classify evidence, not a ladder of proof.
|SMT / proof assistant |No backend dependency, proof artefact or proof-checking
CI gate in the inspected workspace. The roadmap states proof obligations.
|SMT discharge, CEGAR, mechanised transfer soundness, termination and
source-to-model correspondence proofs remain future work.
|===

There are *13 registered source rules*: ten T0 and three T1 (two Python, one
PHP). Trace commands are separate from source-rule coverage. A worklist solver
is an implemented analysis algorithm; the word "solver" does not imply an SMT
engine or theorem prover. Finite monotone domains explain convergence without
widening. That argument does not prove the parser, CFG construction, language
semantics, transfer functions or implementation correct.

Tests, parser mutation smoke runs, killed rule mutants and reduced-model
conformance are engineering evidence. The measured perfect score on the small
labelled regression corpus is not a production precision/recall estimate.
See link:PORTFOLIO-UPGRADE-REPORT.adoc[exact corpus, seeds, measurements and gaps]
and link:../PROOF-PROGRESS.adoc[the milestone ledger].

== What an operator can conclude

* A T0 finding identifies a local candidate. A T1 finding identifies a possible
  flow in the supported abstraction; its path may be infeasible concretely.
* A T2 violation identifies a supplied event sequence inconsistent with the
  stated model. Premises such as signature validity or durable application
  require independent evidence. Passing examples are not universal proofs.
* No findings means no findings from the selected rules on the analysed input.
  Even complete-within-policy coverage does not certify correctness or safety.
  Unsupported, opaque, timed-out or unreadable input remains visible; zero
  applicable input and required-language failures cannot pass the CLI gate.
* PHP syntax support does not imply full WordPress or Composer semantics.
  Haskell and Elixir remain unsupported. Application analysis cannot certify
  the PHP interpreter or establish when hooks run relative to request parsing.

== Opportunities with concrete acceptance gates

. Strengthen the PHP slice first: resolve imported names, Composer/include
  boundaries and callbacks; extend exceptional control flow. Require fixtures
  that distinguish vulnerable, corrected, similar safe and unknown cases.
. Connect source and protocol models narrowly: extract events for one reviewed
  API, declare receiver/alias identity and validate against instrumented traces.
  Retain unknown commands, missing instrumentation and alternate paths.
. Add optional bounded SMT path feasibility for one supported subset. Preserve
  solver/version, model, query, bounds, timeout and counterexample artefacts.
  SAT needs a concrete witness check; UNSAT applies only to the encoded bounded
  query; UNKNOWN or timeout must stay unknown. Do not silently upgrade T0/T1.
. Mechanise a small abstraction: define concrete/abstract semantics, prove
  transfer soundness and worklist convergence, then establish parser/CFG
  correspondence or translation validation. State the trusted computing base.
. Expand independent labelled corpora and adversarial tests. Publish coverage,
  precision/recall and resource limits separately; validate cross-tool report
  consumers without merging their identities or overstating independence.

These are bounded research and engineering opportunities, not a promise of a
universal verifier. A verified model still needs evidence that the application
and deployed environment satisfy its premises.
